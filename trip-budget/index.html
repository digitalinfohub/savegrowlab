What will the trip really cost?

The guide behind the tool

The three costs people forget

Most holiday budgets are built from the exciting numbers: flights and a hotel. The expensive numbers are quieter. Transfers and local transport can add £100 to a week. Food at £35 per person per day for two people for a week is £490 — often more than the flights. Airport extras — parking, lounge, baggage, that £4 water — add another chunk before you have left the country.

Add them all, then add a buffer. A trip that costs £1,400 and was budgeted at £1,200 is not a disaster; it is just a plan that was missing a line item.

Pay for it before you go

A holiday is easier to enjoy when it is already paid for. Work out the total, divide by the months until departure, and set a standing order. For a £1,800 trip in nine months that is £200 a month — use the savings calculator to adjust. Put accommodation on a card only if you can clear it before the statement date; otherwise it is a debt with a sea view.

Cutting the cost without cutting the trip

  • Travel in the shoulder season — the same place, 30% cheaper, fewer crowds.
  • Book accommodation with a kitchen. Two breakfasts out a day is £300 a week.
  • Set a daily cash limit and take it out once. Card taps do not feel like money.
  • Choose one paid activity per day, not three. The free walk is usually the memory.
  • Check cancellation terms before paying — a cheap booking you cannot change is not cheap.

Frequently asked questions

Should the buffer be included in the saving target?

Yes. Save the total including the buffer. If you do not spend it, you have started the next trip’s fund — or a small treat.

How much spending money per day is realistic?

£30–£50 per person per day for food and local transport is a reasonable UK/Europe planning figure outside major cities. City breaks need more.

Do I include travel insurance?

Yes, and put it in activities/extras. It is small compared to the cost of needing it.

Can I use this for a UK staycation?

Absolutely. Set transport to fuel or train fare, accommodation to the cottage, and food to self-catering costs.
