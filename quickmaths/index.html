Quick maths

Four money sums, done in seconds.

The everyday maths that actually saves money: split a bill fairly, check whether a discount is real, compare price per unit, and estimate the basket before you reach the checkout.

The guide behind the tool

Splitting a bill without the awkward pause

Add the tip to the total, divide by the number of people. Paying £120 with a 10% tip across four people is £33 each. If one person had the steak and two had starters, an even split is convenient but not fair — decide before the bill arrives, not after.

On a phone, this is the calculation nobody wants to do aloud. Keep this page open at the table. The budget planner is where the meal should have been accounted for.

Is the discount real?

“30% off” is a fact about the price, not about the value. A £80 jacket at 30% off is £56 — a saving of £24 if you were going to buy it anyway, and a cost of £56 if you were not. Stacked codes apply to the already-reduced price, not the original, which is why 30% then 10% is 37% off, not 40%. The calculator gets that right so you do not have to.

The only good-deal test: would you buy it at this price if there were no countdown timer? If not, the discount is doing the selling.

Unit price: the supermarket’s quiet trick

Bigger packs usually look cheaper and sometimes are not. Divide the price by the quantity and compare like for like. £3.50 for 500g is £7 per kilo; £5.50 for 1kg is £5.50 per kilo — the bigger pack wins, but only by £1.50. On a product you use weekly, that adds up to about £78 a year.

The basket estimate

Before you queue, count the items and multiply by a rough price. Nine items at £4 is £36. The number will be wrong — that is the point. Comparing your estimate with the till total tells you whether your sense of “a small shop” is calibrated. Most people find it is 30–50% low. Repeat it weekly and the gap closes, which is free money.

Frequently asked questions

Does the split include the tip before dividing?

Yes. The tip is calculated on the bill, added to the total, then split evenly between people.

How do stacked discounts work?

Each percentage applies to the already-discounted price. 30% off then 10% off is 37% off overall, not 40%.

What unit should I use for unit price?

Whatever both products share: grams, millilitres, sheets or items. The calculator only compares the resulting price per unit.

Why is my basket estimate always low?

Because you are estimating the average item, and the expensive items are the ones you forget. Add 20% to the estimate as a personal buffer.
