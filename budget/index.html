The guide behind the tool

Why the 50/30/20 rule still works

It answers the only budgeting question that matters on payday: how much can leave before the month starts? Essentials (needs) take half, lifestyle (wants) takes 30%, and 20% goes to saving or debt overpayment. It is simple enough to remember without opening an app and strict enough to build a cushion.

In the UK, though, housing is the spoiler. If rent and Council Tax alone clear 50% of your pay, a literal 50/30/20 split is not a discipline problem — it is arithmetic. That is why the planner offers 60/20/20 and 70/15/15. A framework you can keep beats a perfect one you quit in March.

Watch the leak: subscriptions and delivery apps usually live in lifestyle but behave like essentials — they leave automatically. Audit them once a quarter with the subscription audit tool.

The two-minute payday routine

  1. Salary lands. Same day: standing order moves the saving amount to a separate account.
  2. Fixed bills leave by direct debit as normal.
  3. Divide what remains by the weeks until payday. That weekly number is your true spending limit.
  4. Once a month, check the split — not every purchase. Budgeting by guilt is unsustainable.

If essentials are over 70%, the fix is structural rather than behavioural: renegotiate a bill, change a commute, or raise income. No spreadsheet closes a £400 housing gap. Our UK budgeting guide walks through a full example, and the 50/30/20 deep dive covers the variants.

What to do in your first month

Do not try to categorise every coffee. Set the saving transfer, keep essentials on direct debit, and give yourself a weekly lifestyle number. At the end of the month, compare the plan to reality once. Adjust one line, not ten. Repeat: that is the whole system.

Frequently asked questions

Is 20% saving realistic on a UK salary?

Not always. Start at 5–10% and escalate when income rises. The point of the planner is to show the size of each slice, not to make you feel behind.

Should debt payments count as essentials or saving?

Minimum payments are essentials. Anything above the minimum is saving — it buys future interest back. Use the debt planner to see the effect.

Does the planner include pension contributions?

No. Use take-home pay after pension. Your workplace pension is already saving, and it is worth checking the employer match separately.

Can I use this if my income changes each month?

Yes — budget from your lowest reliable month, and treat anything above it as bonus money to split between saving and lifestyle.
