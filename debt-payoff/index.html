The guide behind the tool

Why minimum payments feel endless

Card minimum payments are usually a small percentage of the balance, which means they fall as you pay it down. The balance shrinks, so the payment shrinks, so the balance shrinks more slowly. On a £3,000 balance at 22.9% APR, paying only the minimum can take well over a decade and cost more in interest than the original spend.

The fix is mechanical: pay a fixed amount, not the minimum. The calculator assumes a fixed payment, which is the honest way to plan.

Snowball or avalanche?

MethodOrderBest for
AvalancheHighest APR firstSaving the most money
SnowballSmallest balance firstStaying motivated with quick wins
ConsolidationOne lower-rate loanOnly if the rate is genuinely lower and fees are small

Mathematically the avalanche wins. Behaviourally the snowball often wins, because a cleared balance is proof the system works. If you have several debts, pick the method you will actually finish; the difference in interest is usually smaller than the cost of quitting.

Do not borrow to invest while carrying high-rate debt. A guaranteed 22.9% saving on interest beats an uncertain 6% investment return.

Finding the extra payment

Most people find the overpayment in three places: subscriptions they stopped using (run the audit), a phone contract that is out of term, and the weekly lifestyle number. £50 a month found in those three places can cut a card payoff by years.

Keep a small emergency buffer while you pay debt. Without one, the next car repair goes back on the card and you undo the progress. The emergency fund calculator sizes that buffer, and the full comparison guide goes deeper.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include credit card fees?

No. It models interest only. Annual fees and late charges make the real cost higher.

Why does the calculator say I will never clear it?

If the monthly payment is less than the monthly interest, the balance cannot fall. Increase the payment above that threshold first.

Should I clear debt before saving?

Keep a small starter buffer, then prioritise any debt above roughly 10% APR. Guaranteed interest saved usually beats uncertain investment returns.

Can I use this for a mortgage?

Technically yes, but mortgages are long and have early-repayment charges. Use it for cards, overdrafts, store finance and personal loans.
