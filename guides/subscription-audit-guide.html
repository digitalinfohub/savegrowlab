Home›Guides›The subscription audit: what are you actually paying…
Guide · Budgeting · 6 min read · Updated 10 November 2026

Subscriptions are the most profitable business model in consumer finance because they rely on one thing: you forgetting. The service is cheap enough to ignore and automatic enough to keep charging. An audit takes twenty minutes and usually finds more than a month of savings.

Step 1 — find every one

Open your bank app and search for repeating payments over the last two months. Then check three places people miss:

  • Annual plans — search your email for “renewal”, “receipt” or the name of the service.
  • App stores — phone subscriptions are billed by the store, not the merchant.
  • Free trials — the ones you meant to cancel and did not.

Write the list down before deciding anything. The total is the motivation.

Step 2 — triage each one

QuestionIf yesIf no
Would I buy this again today at this price?Keep it, consciouslyCancel it now
Do I use it at least weekly?Keep, check pricePause or cancel
Is it duplicated by another service or account?Cancel the more expensiveKeep
Is it still in a minimum term?Set a cancellation reminderCancel today

Step 3 — kill the trial trap

Every free trial gets a phone reminder two days before it ends. If a service will not let you cancel online, cancel by email and keep the confirmation. In the UK, many services must offer a clear cancellation route — but relying on the law is slower than setting a reminder.

Price rises are silent. Check the renewal email for the new price, not the old one. A service that rose from £8 to £12 is a 50% increase you did not consciously agree to.

Step 4 — use the savings

Cancel two £12 services and you have £288 a year. That is not “small”. Send it to a named goal with the savings calculator, or use it to start the sinking fund for next Christmas. If the money simply disappears into the current account, the audit will need repeating in six months.

Repeat the whole exercise quarterly. It takes five minutes once you have a list, and it catches the trials and price rises that creep in between.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find annual subscriptions?

Search your email for renewal receipts and check app store subscription settings. Annual plans rarely show up in a monthly bank scan.

Should I cancel everything?

No. Keep what you genuinely use — the point is deliberate spending, not austerity.

What if I am still in a contract?

Check the early cancellation terms and set a reminder for the renewal date. Cancelling early can cost more than waiting.

How often should I audit?

Every three months, plus always two days before a free trial ends.
