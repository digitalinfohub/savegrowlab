Guide · Borrowing · 8 min read · Updated 10 November 2026

Two methods dominate debt payoff advice. The avalanche attacks the highest interest rate first. The snowball attacks the smallest balance first. Both require the same core behaviour: pay minimums on everything, then throw every spare pound at one debt.

A worked comparison

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Store card£40029.9%£20
Credit card£2,50021.9%£75
Personal loan£4,0008.9%£160

With £150 extra a month, the avalanche clears the store card first because its rate is highest, then the card, then the loan. The snowball also clears the store card first — it happens to be the smallest — so on this set they agree. Remove the store card and they diverge: avalanche targets the 21.9% card, snowball targets the smaller loan balance if it were smaller.

The difference in total interest is usually a few hundred pounds on typical UK balances. Meaningful, but smaller than the difference between finishing and quitting.

How to choose

  • Choose avalanche if you are motivated by efficiency and the highest rate is a card charging 25%+.
  • Choose snowball if you have several small balances and need a visible win in the first two months.
  • Either way, pay a fixed amount rather than the minimum. Minimums fall as the balance falls, which stretches the payoff.
Do not consolidate without checking the rate. Moving a 29.9% card to a 0% balance transfer can help, but a fee of 3–5% plus a return to a high rate later can cost more than the original debt. Read the terms and the fee, not just the headline rate.

When to get free help

If payments are already being missed or you are borrowing to cover essentials, stop optimising and get advice. In the UK, StepChange, National Debtline and MoneyHelper all provide free, non-judgemental guidance. Ignoring a debt is the expensive option; talking to a free service is not.

Keep a small buffer while you pay

Without £500 set aside, the next car repair goes back on the card and months of progress vanish. Build a starter buffer first, then attack the debt, then return to a full emergency fund. The emergency fund calculator sizes it, and the debt planner shows what an extra payment saves.

Frequently asked questions

Which method saves more money?

The avalanche, because it targets the highest interest rate. The gap depends on your balances and rates.

Does the snowball cost more?

Usually a little more in interest, but it clears a balance sooner, which many people need in order to keep going.

Should I pay off my mortgage first?

No. Mortgages are relatively cheap and often have early repayment charges. Clear expensive consumer debt first.

Can I negotiate with lenders?

Yes. If you are struggling, contact lenders early and ask about a payment plan or breathing space. Free debt charities can help you do it.
