Guide · Earning · 13 min read · Updated 18 November 2026

Cutting costs has a floor. Income does not.

You can only save so much from a grocery bill. A pay rise, a better-paid move or a small side income changes the whole equation — and unlike most money advice, it compounds with every future pay review. This guide is about the realistic version, including the tax rules people discover too late.

  • When to move jobs, and how to price the move honestly.
  • Side income that fits around a job, with realistic earnings ranges.
  • The £1,000 trading allowance, self-assessment triggers and benefit interactions.

The three income levers

Almost every increase comes from one of three places: more per hour (a raise, promotion, or switch to a better-paying employer), more hours (overtime, a second job), or a new stream (freelancing, selling, renting something out). Each has a different cost in time, risk and energy. The mistake is chasing all three at once and doing none of them well.

Asking for a pay rise

Timing does most of the work. Ask when budgets are being set, not after they are spent — often a few months before the end of the financial year, or immediately after a visible win. Ask in person or on a call, not in a passing message. And ask for a specific number with a reason, not for “a review”.

Build a one-page evidence pack before the meeting:

  • Your responsibilities now versus when your salary was set. New systems, clients, people or scope all count.
  • Three concrete outcomes with numbers — revenue, cost saved, time saved, retention, delivery speed.
  • Market evidence: two or three comparable roles with salary ranges, from job ads or a salary survey. Bring the range, not a single hopeful figure.
  • What you are asking for, and the date you would like it to apply from.
“I would like to discuss my salary. Since March I have taken on the supplier reporting and cut the monthly close from six days to three. Comparable roles are advertised at £38,000–£42,000, and I am asking for £40,000 from April. If that is not possible now, I would like to agree what would make it possible by the next review.”

That script does four things: it is specific, it is evidenced, it names a number, and it stays in the room if the answer is no. The last part matters — a good manager can work with a documented target; they cannot work with resentment.

Expect one of four responses: yes, a smaller number, “not now”, or a deflection. For a smaller number, ask what would close the gap and when. For “not now”, ask for a date and the criteria in writing. For a flat no with no reason, you have learned something useful about your future there.

Moving jobs without wrecking your life

Switching employer is often the fastest route to a meaningful increase because internal budgets are anchored to your current salary while the market is not. Price the move properly: a 15% rise that adds £250 a month in commuting and childcare is not a 15% rise. Include pension contribution differences, holiday, notice period, and the risk of probation.

Do not accept a counter-offer reflexively. Sometimes it is genuine; often it buys time while your employer plans a replacement. If you would have left for the new role at the same money, the counter-offer is not the point.

Side income that actually pays

The realistic side incomes share three traits: they use a skill you already have, they can be sold repeatedly, and the setup cost is near zero. Be wary of anything that requires buying stock or paying to join.

Rent a Room relief currently lets you receive up to £7,500 a year from a lodger tax-free, under specific conditions. It is one of the few genuinely generous allowances left, and it requires you to actually share your home.

Tax, allowances and the paperwork

The trading allowance lets you receive up to £1,000 a year gross from self-employment without registering for Self Assessment or paying tax on it, provided you do not deduct expenses as well. Above that, you register and file. Do not treat £1,000 as a target — it is a threshold, and crossing it means paperwork, not a penalty.

  • Register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you started. Filing and payment deadlines follow in January.
  • Keep records of income and allowable expenses from day one. A separate bank account makes this trivial.
  • Class 4 National Insurance may apply to profits above the lower profits threshold; the rules and thresholds change, so check the current figures.
  • If you receive Universal Credit or tax credits, extra earnings affect your award — report changes promptly rather than at the end of the year.
  • Check your employment contract for exclusivity or conflict-of-interest clauses before starting anything related to your day job.
Check before you earn: a side income can reduce means-tested benefits, student finance entitlements or visa-compliant hours. The tax is usually the smaller surprise.

Scams and traps

  • Pay-to-start “opportunities”. Legitimate work pays you; it does not charge you for training, stock or a position.
  • Guaranteed returns. There is no such thing outside a savings account within FSCS limits. Crypto “staking” and forex “mentors” are the usual vehicles.
  • Upfront fees for a job. Never pay for a DBS check, equipment or onboarding before a signed contract.
  • “Be your own boss” schemes. If the money comes from recruiting others rather than selling a product, it is a pyramid structure with extra steps.

Earning more: questions

How much can I earn before paying tax on a side income?

The trading allowance lets you receive up to £1,000 a year gross from self-employment without registering or paying tax, if you do not also claim expenses. Above that, normal income tax and possibly National Insurance apply. The allowance is not a loophole for employment income.

Do I need to tell my employer?

Check your contract for exclusivity, conflict-of-interest and confidentiality clauses. Many allow outside work that does not compete. If in doubt, ask before you start rather than after.

Will a side income affect my benefits?

It can. Universal Credit and tax credits take earnings into account, usually with a taper. Report changes promptly and check your position with the benefit office or a free adviser before relying on the extra income.

How do I invoice as a sole trader?

A simple invoice with your name, address, invoice number, date, description, amount and payment terms is enough. Keep copies and a log of expenses. You do not need a limited company to start.

What is the fastest realistic income increase?

For most employees, a well-prepared pay review or an employer switch beats a side hustle in hourly terms. A side income is better for flexibility and skill-building than for a quick cash injection.
