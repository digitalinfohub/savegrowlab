Home›Guides›A cheaper holiday, without a worse holiday
Guide · Saving · 7 min read · Updated 10 November 2026

Holidays are not a waste of money — they are one of the few purchases with a reliable happiness return. But the price varies enormously for the same trip depending on when you book, where you stay and how you spend while you are there.

Timing beats everything

School holidays and bank-holiday weekends carry a premium that no amount of comparison shopping can remove. Two weeks earlier or later can cut accommodation by 30–40%. If your dates are fixed, the saving has to come from elsewhere — so decide which is more flexible: the date or the destination. One of them should be.

Accommodation and food

  • Self-catering costs more per night but saves on breakfast and lunch. Two breakfasts out a day is roughly £300 a week for two people.
  • Location matters more than star rating. A 15-minute walk from the centre often saves the cost of a nicer hotel over a week.
  • Direct booking can beat platforms, especially for small independents — but keep the cancellation terms in writing.
  • One paid activity a day. Three paid attractions a day is how a budget holiday becomes an expensive one.

A daily spending rule

Set a daily cash limit and take it out once. Card taps do not feel like money; a limited wallet does. For a UK or European trip, £30–£50 per person per day covers food and local transport outside major cities. City breaks need more.

Include the boring costs: airport parking, baggage, travel insurance, transfers, roaming and a buffer. These are the lines that turn a £1,200 budget into a £1,450 bill.

Save before you book

The cheapest holiday is the one you have already paid for. Cost the trip with the holiday budget calculator, add 10–15%, divide by the months until departure and automate the transfer. A £1,800 trip in nine months is £200 a month — the savings calculator will check whether that fits your other goals.

Avoid putting the trip on a card you cannot clear before the statement date. A holiday that takes a year to pay off costs more than the holiday and lasts longer than the tan.

Do not skip insurance

Travel insurance is small compared with the cost of a medical bill or a cancelled trip. Check whether your bank account or home insurance already includes cover before buying a separate policy — and if it does, remember that you are already paying for it. That is a subscription worth reviewing in the audit.

Frequently asked questions

When is the cheapest time to book flights?

There is no universal rule, but for short-haul European travel, booking roughly 6–12 weeks ahead and avoiding school holidays is usually better than last-minute.

Is it cheaper to book a package?

Sometimes, especially for families with fixed dates. Compare the package total against separate flights and accommodation, including transfers.

How much buffer should I add?

10–15% of the base cost. It covers transfers, a pricey meal and the taxi you did not plan.

Should I use a credit card for protection?

Section 75 protection applies to purchases over £100 on credit cards, which is a genuine benefit — but only if you clear the balance before interest starts.

Price the trip properly.

Add transport, accommodation, food and a buffer to see the real total and the monthly saving.
