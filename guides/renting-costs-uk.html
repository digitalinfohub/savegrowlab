The real cost of renting

Adverts quote the rent. The tenancy costs considerably more: a deposit to find in one go, a month's rent in advance, a van, a bed, and a set of bills that start the day you get the keys. This is the full number, before and after you move in.

What this guide covers

  • What you need on the day you sign, and what can no longer be charged.
  • How deposit protection works, and how to get the money back.
  • A realistic monthly running-cost budget including the bills renters forget.
  • The hidden costs written into tenancy agreements.

The upfront cost

Assume three things are due in the same fortnight: a deposit, the first month's rent, and everything needed to actually live there. On a flat at £950 a month, the bill looks like this.

ItemTypical amountNotes

Tenancy deposit£1,096Five weeks' rent maximum in England and Wales.
First month's rent£950Sometimes the first month plus a part-month if you move mid-cycle.
Referencing and admin£0Banned in England and Wales. If you are charged, query it in writing.
Van or removals£250More for a long distance, stairs or a lot of furniture.
Bed, sofa, white goods£400–£1,200Unfurnished flats are common; check what is actually included.
Council tax, first instalment£120Band and discount dependent. Set up before the first bill.

Contents insurance, first year£120Optional but cheap; see the insurance guide.
Total before keys£3,400–£4,200Of which roughly £1,300 is refundable deposit.

That is why moving is often a two-payday project. If you are leaving a previous tenancy, the old deposit usually comes back after you move out — not before — so plan for the gap unless your landlord agrees an early release.

Fees that are banned

Since 2019, letting agents in England and Wales cannot charge most of the fees that used to pad out a move. What a landlord or agent can still ask for:

  • Rent.
  • A refundable tenancy deposit, capped at five weeks' rent where annual rent is under £50,000.
  • A refundable holding deposit, capped at one week's rent.
  • Payments for utilities, broadband, TV licence and council tax that you use under the tenancy.
  • A default fee for a late payment or a lost key, and only if the tenancy agreement says so and the amount is reasonable.
  • Payments for a breach you caused, or a variation you requested.

Referencing, inventory, check-in, "admin" and renewals are not on that list. If an agent asks for one, ask them to put the charge in writing and quote the legislation — most charges disappear at that point, and a complaint to the local authority trading standards team is the escalation route.

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own rules. Scottish tenancies have different notice and deposit rules again, so check the current guidance for the nation you are renting in.

Deposit protection

Your deposit is not the landlord's money to hold freely. In England and Wales it must be protected with a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receipt, and you must be given the prescribed information about where it is held.

  • Three schemes: Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits and the Tenancy Deposit Scheme.
  • Custodial means the scheme holds the money. Insured means the landlord holds it and pays for insurance.
  • If it is not protected within the deadline, the landlord can be ordered to repay it and pay compensation of between one and three times the deposit.

Check the protection yourself rather than taking it on trust: each scheme has a free online checker. Do it in the first month, while the paperwork is still easy to find. If the tenancy ends and the deposit was never protected, the claim does not expire with the tenancy.

The monthly budget

Rent is usually about 60–70% of a renter's housing cost. The rest arrives as separate bills, and several of them are new to people moving out of home.

Two checks worth doing before you sign: ask which council tax band the property is in and look it up on the council's site, and ask for the last 12 months of energy use. An EPC rating tells you the theoretical cost; the previous bills tell you the real one. A cheap flat with electric heating can cost more per month than a slightly dearer one with gas.

Hidden costs in the agreement

  • Bills included sounds simple until the landlord caps usage or the arrangement ends mid-tenancy. Get the cap in writing.
  • Communal heating in a block is often charged through a separate standing charge you cannot switch.
  • Parking permits can be £50–£500 a year depending on the council and the zone.
  • Garden maintenance is sometimes the tenant's responsibility, including tools.
  • Break clauses usually work both ways: the landlord can end the tenancy at the break date too.
  • Early termination can leave you paying rent until a replacement tenant is found, plus the landlord's re-letting costs if the agreement allows it.
  • Furniture left by a previous tenant is not always yours to keep or remove — check the inventory.

A guarantor is another cost people underestimate. If a landlord asks for one, the guarantor is liable for your rent if you do not pay, and they need to be a homeowner in many cases. If you have no guarantor, some landlords accept a larger deposit or several months' rent upfront — which is a cash-flow problem disguised as a condition.

Getting the deposit back

Most deposit disputes are about cleaning and damage, and most are avoidable with photographs. The sequence that works:

  1. Photograph everything on day one. Every wall, appliance, scratch and stain. Email the set to the agent so it is timestamped.
  2. Check the inventory within the first week and return it with comments. Silence is usually taken as agreement.
  3. Report repairs in writing as they happen. Untreated damp or a broken extractor fan becomes "tenant damage" at the end of the tenancy.
  4. Clean to the standard in the agreement, not to a professional standard unless it says so. "Professional cleaning" clauses are not automatically enforceable.
  5. Attend the check-out if you can, and photograph the property in the same state.

If you agree the deductions, the balance should be returned within 10 days in England and Wales. If you disagree, the scheme's free dispute service decides — you do not need a solicitor, and the money stays protected while it is resolved. Fair wear and tear is not chargeable; a landlord cannot bill you for a carpet that has simply aged.

Renting questions

Can a letting agent charge a referencing fee?

Not in England or Wales. Most letting fees were banned in 2019, including referencing, inventory and admin charges. Rent, a refundable deposit, a holding deposit and utility-type payments are still allowed.

How much deposit can a landlord ask for?

Five weeks' rent in England and Wales where annual rent is under £50,000, and two months' rent in Scotland. A holding deposit is capped at one week and must be credited to your rent or deposit.

When should I get my deposit back?

Within 10 days of agreeing the amount in England and Wales. If you disagree, the protection scheme's free arbitration decides, and the money stays protected until it does.

Is contents insurance worth it as a renter?

Usually yes. The landlord insures the building, not your belongings. A basic policy with accidental damage costs a few pounds a week and covers the loss a fire or leak would otherwise leave with you.

Can my rent go up mid-tenancy?

Usually only in the way the agreement allows, and tenancy law in this area has been changing. Check the current rules for your nation with Shelter or GOV.UK before agreeing to an increase.

Not advice. Tenancy law differs across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and the rules on tenancy types and rent increases have been changing. Check GOV.UK and Shelter for the current position before you sign.
