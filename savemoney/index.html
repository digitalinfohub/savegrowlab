Savings goal calculator

How much do I need to save each month?

Enter a target, what you have already, and your deadline. You get the exact monthly amount — plus how long your current pace would take. Everything is calculated on your phone or laptop, not on a server.

The guide behind the tool

What this calculator is actually doing

The maths is deliberately boring: take the gap between your target and what you have already saved, then divide it by the months you have left. If you add an interest rate, the calculator solves the same sum backwards so the interest your money earns along the way reduces what you need to contribute.

With a 0% rate it is exactly (target − saved) ÷ months. At 4% over three years the monthly figure is meaningfully smaller, because the balance starts earning on itself. If you want to see why that happens, the compound interest calculator shows the curve.

A worked example

You want £1,500 for a holiday in 12 months and have £300 already. Without interest you need £100 a month. Add the £150 you can actually spare and the calculator says you will get there in 8 months instead — six months earlier than the deadline. That is the useful bit: the same numbers answer both “how much?” and “how long?”.

The deadline is negotiable. If the required monthly amount is painful, stretch the timeline before you abandon the goal. £1,500 over 12 months is £125; over 18 months it is £83. One extra season of waiting often makes a plan survive contact with real life.

How to make the number real

  • Move it on payday. A standing order for the day after your salary lands beats a promise to transfer “what is left”.
  • Use a separate account. Money attached to your debit card gets spent. A transfer that takes a day to reverse is a feature.
  • Name the pot. “Japan trip” is harder to raid than “Savings”.
  • Save the plan. Press the save button and Penny will remember the target on this device.
  • Check in monthly. Two minutes on the first Sunday is enough to adjust the figure.

If the monthly amount still does not fit, fix the budget first. The budget planner shows where the money should come from; the saving guide covers the habit side.

Three mistakes this tool prevents

1. Saving for a round number instead of the real cost. A £1,200 holiday usually costs £1,500 once food, transfers and a buffer are included.

2. Ignoring money you already have. Starting from zero when £300 is already set aside makes the plan look harder than it is.

3. Using an interest rate you cannot get. Be conservative. A 5% assumption on a one-year goal is optimism, not planning.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator store my numbers?

No. The arithmetic runs inside your browser and nothing is sent anywhere. If you press “Save this plan”, the target and monthly amount are stored in your browser’s local storage so Penny can use them next time — you can clear that by clearing site data.

Should I include interest for a goal under two years?

Usually not, or only a small amount. Interest on short horizons barely changes the monthly figure, and savings rates can fall. Use 0% for anything under 12 months.

What if I miss a month?

Recalculate rather than give up. Spread the missed amount across the remaining months or extend the deadline by one. A plan that bends is a plan that survives.

Can I save for several goals at once?

Yes — use the goal comparison tool to see what several goals cost together, and what to drop if the total is over budget.
