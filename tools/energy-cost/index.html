The guide behind the tool

A bill is three numbers, not one

Every domestic energy bill is built from the same parts: a standing charge you pay whatever you use, a unit rate for each kWh consumed, and VAT at 5% for domestic supplies. Suppliers quote the first two separately because they move independently — a cheap unit rate with a high standing charge is expensive for a low user and cheap for a family.

That is why "the cheapest tariff" is not a single answer. If you use very little gas because you heat with electricity, a tariff with a low standing charge wins even if the unit rate is average. If you use a lot, the unit rate dominates. This calculator shows both lines so the comparison is honest.

Gas boiler15–30 kW when firingHeating and hot water usually dominate the gas bill.

To cost a single appliance: kilowatts × hours used × unit rate in pence ÷ 100. A 2 kW dryer run for 90 minutes at a 25p unit rate is 2 × 1.5 × 25 ÷ 100 = 75p, plus its share of the standing charge.

The standing charge nobody chooses

The standing charge covers network costs, meter maintenance and, in part, the cost of supplier failures. It is charged every day of the year whether the property is occupied or not, which is why an empty home still costs money and why a low-usage household can feel overcharged.

It is not negotiable on a standard tariff, but it is a legitimate comparison point when switching: two tariffs with the same unit rate but different standing charges produce different annual totals. Multiply the daily figure by 365 and add VAT to see the real annual cost.

What actually reduces the bill

  • Heat the person, not the house. A smaller heated zone beats a whole-house setback on cost per comfort unit.
  • Fix the draughts before the thermostat. Draught-proofing and loft insulation are usually the cheapest kWh you will ever not buy.
  • Shift heavy use. If your tariff has a cheaper overnight window, move the washing machine and dishwasher into it.
  • Check the boiler flow temperature. Lower flow temperatures often heat the home just as well for less gas.
  • Read the meter monthly. Estimated bills hide both overspend and overpayment; a reading every month keeps the direct debit honest.

Frequently asked questions

Is VAT included?

Yes, the result adds 5% VAT, which is the domestic rate for gas and electricity. Business supplies are treated differently.

Why is my bill higher in winter?

Usage, not the rate. Heating and lighting rise sharply from October to March, while the standing charge stays constant. Compare months of similar weather, not December with June.

Does the standing charge apply if I use nothing?

Yes, while the supply is connected. Some suppliers offer zero-standing-charge tariffs with higher unit rates; whether that is cheaper depends entirely on your usage.

What is a typical household usage?

Regulator figures exist for low, medium and high usage, but they are averages. Use your own meter readings — the calculator is only as good as the numbers you put in.

Can I be cut off for not paying?

Suppliers must follow strict rules before disconnecting domestic customers, and offer payment plans first. Contact the supplier and any available support scheme before arrears build, not after.
