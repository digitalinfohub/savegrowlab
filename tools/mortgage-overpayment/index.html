Mortgage overpayment calculator

The guide behind the tool

Why a small overpayment beats a big one later

Mortgage interest is charged on the balance that exists, so every pound repaid early stops earning interest for every month that follows. An overpayment in year two saves interest for twenty years; the same money in year twenty saves almost nothing. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why consistency matters more than size.

The calculator runs your mortgage twice — once at the contractual payment, once with the overpayment added — and reports the difference in total interest and in months. If the gap looks implausible, check the rate: a small change in the assumed rate moves the answer a long way over a long term.

The allowance is the thing to check first

Most fixed and discounted deals allow overpayments up to a percentage of the balance each year without charge — commonly 10%, sometimes 5%. Above that, an early repayment charge applies, usually as a percentage of the excess, tapering towards the end of the fix.

  • The allowance is normally per mortgage year, resetting on the deal anniversary.
  • Regular monthly overpayments and lump sums usually share the same allowance.
  • Charges sit in the mortgage offer document, not on the annual statement.

If the charge exceeds the interest saved, wait. Six weeks can be worth a percentage point of the balance.

Term or payment reduction

Two overpayments of the same size can produce very different outcomes depending on what you ask the lender to do. Reducing the term keeps the payment and finishes earlier — the maximum interest saving. Reducing the payment keeps the term and lowers the monthly cost — useful if the overpayment was really about creating breathing room.

This calculator assumes the term reduces, which is the common default and the cheaper option. If your lender defaults to reducing the payment instead, ask them to change it.

What to do before overpaying

  1. Hold one month of essential costs in an instant-access account. An overpayment cannot be reversed quickly.
  2. Clear anything costing more than the mortgage rate — cards, car finance, buy-now-pay-later balances.
  3. Take the full employer pension match. It is an immediate return no mortgage can match.
  4. Fund the irregular costs — car, boiler, dentist — through sinking funds rather than the mortgage.

The full reasoning, including the savings-versus-overpayment comparison, is in the mortgage overpayment guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is the result exact?

No. It assumes the rate stays fixed and interest is charged monthly on the reducing balance. Your lender recalculates after rate changes, and daily interest produces small differences.

Does overpaying reduce my monthly payment?

Only if you ask. The default at many lenders is to keep the payment and shorten the term, which saves more interest.

Can I overpay a lump sum instead?

Yes, and the calculator's monthly figure is a reasonable stand-in for the same annual total. Pay the lump sum as early in the mortgage year as possible to maximise the saving.

What about an offset mortgage?

An offset lets savings reduce the interest charged without repaying capital, so the money stays accessible. It usually costs a slightly higher rate — worth it if liquidity matters more than the last fraction of interest.

Should I overpay while I have a cheap fix?

Compare the fix rate with what your savings earn after tax. If savings pay more, keep the cash; the difference is small and liquidity has value.
