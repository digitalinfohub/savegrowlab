Make the easy money decisions easier.

Small, consistent systems reduce financial friction without turning every daily purchase into a stressful spreadsheet exercise.

01 · Pause

Use a 24-hour rule

For non-essential purchases over a certain amount, wait a full day before deciding. You can still buy it, but the decision becomes deliberate rather than impulsive, saving you from buyer's remorse.

Good for impulse buys
02 · Automate

Move savings after payday

Choose a manageable, painless amount and set up an automatic transfer into a dedicated savings account on payday. Pay yourself first, rather than waiting to see what is left at the end of the month.

Consistency beats intensity
03 · Total it up

Watch the basket, not one item

Several "small" purchases can quickly become a large total. A quick mental estimate keeps the whole basket visible and prevents budget creep at the supermarket or online.

Use the £5 rule
04 · Recurring

Audit subscriptions quarterly

Review recurring payments regularly. Label each as essential, useful, or unused before the next renewal. Canceling just two unused subscriptions can save hundreds per year.

Low effort, high reward
05 · Goals

Name the reason

"£600 for Japan" is far more actionable and motivating than a vague "save more" goal. Give your money a specific target and track the progress visually.

Make it measurable
06 · Friction

Make expensive habits harder

Remove saved payment details from browsers, turn off promotional notifications, or use a wishlist. Adding a small speed bump gives your rational brain time to catch up with your emotional brain.

Add a speed bump

Understand the basics before chasing returns.

Learn how common investment vehicles work, what risk truly means, and why time horizon and diversification are your most powerful assets.

Cash

Savings accounts

Useful for short-term goals and emergency funds. Interest provides a return without daily market price movements, though inflation can erode purchasing power over long periods.

Lower volatility
Funds

Funds & ETFs

A single fund can hold hundreds or thousands of underlying investments, making diversification easy and cost-effective. Always check the ongoing charges figure (OCF) and underlying holdings.

Diversification
Shares

Individual companies

Owning shares in one company gives direct exposure to its performance. This can lead to higher returns, but also carries significant company-specific risk and volatility.

Higher concentration
Debt

Bonds

Bonds are debt instruments issued by governments or corporations. They have their own interest-rate, credit, and maturity characteristics, often acting as a stabilizer in a portfolio.

Different risk profile
Speculative

Crypto & alternative assets

Some assets can move sharply in either direction. Large losses are entirely possible, and higher volatility is not the same as a guaranteed higher return. Only allocate what you can afford to lose.

High volatility
Rule

Time changes the picture

Longer investment horizons can smooth out short-term market volatility, but they do not remove the possibility of loss. Never invest money you will need in the next 3-5 years.

Think in years

Interactive tool

Savings pace comparison tool.

Most planning tools assume you have one goal. Real life has several — a car repair fund, a holiday, a deposit, a wedding — all competing for the same monthly surplus. Enter up to four goals below and compare what each demands, together and separately.

Blank or zero rows are ignored. Interest is optional — leave it at 0 for current accounts.

How to read the results

The tool answers three questions at once:

  • What does each goal cost per month? Using the same maths as our Savings Goal calculator: (target − starting balance), grown or reduced by any interest you entered, divided across the months remaining.
  • Do they fit together? The combined monthly demand is compared against the saving budget you set at the top. If the total overshoots, the tool tells you by how much.
  • Which goal is most efficient? The comparison highlights the goal that reaches its target soonest relative to its size, and the one eating the largest share of your budget — usually the right places to start trimming or renegotiating deadlines.
Planning tip: if the combined demand exceeds your budget, don't abandon goals — stretch timelines first. A £1,200 goal over 12 months costs £100/month; over 18 months it costs £67. Deadlines are negotiable far more often than we assume. For the habit side of this, read how to save money consistently.

Worked example

Against a £250 budget, this plan overshoots by about £16 a month — roughly one takeaway. Either extend the holiday by two months or trim the rate assumption, and everything fits. Small adjustments beat abandoned plans.

Does this tool remember my goals?

No. Like every SaveGrow Lab calculator it runs entirely in your browser — refresh the page and the fields reset. Nothing is transmitted or stored anywhere else. See our Privacy Policy.

Why include interest at all if it's small?

On short horizons interest barely moves the number, but on multi-year goals it can cut the required saving noticeably. Our compound interest guide shows exactly when it starts to matter.

Should I fund all goals at once?

Only if the maths above says yes. A common, sane order is: small emergency buffer → matched employer pension contributions → high-cost debt → the rest by deadline urgency. The emergency fund guide explains why the buffer comes first.

Where do ISAs fit?

If a goal is beyond ~18 months and you're using your tax-free allowance, an ISA wrapper may beat a regular account. Our comparison of the mechanics is in ISAs vs savings accounts.

Is SaveGrow Lab financial advice?

No. The site is intended strictly for general education and illustrative calculations. It does not constitute personalised financial advice. A personal recommendation should always take your own specific circumstances into account.

Are the calculators exact?

No. They are planning tools. Interest calculations use simplified assumptions. Real-world interest rates, platform fees, and market returns will differ from these illustrative models.

Can I use the tools on mobile?

Yes. The layout, calculators, and article widgets are fully responsive and designed to work seamlessly on smaller screens.

Do you collect my financial data?

No. We do not require an account, and all calculators run locally in your browser using JavaScript. Your inputs are never sent to our servers.

How accurate is the compound interest calculator?

It applies a constant annual rate compounded monthly with fixed monthly contributions. That is a good approximation for cash savings and regular investing plans, but actual returns vary and investments can fall as well as rise.

How much should a UK beginner aim to keep as an emergency fund?

A common starting target is one month of essential costs, building gradually towards three to six months. Self-employed people or those with variable income often aim higher. Use the to convert that target into a monthly saving amount.
