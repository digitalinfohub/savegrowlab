The guide behind the tool

Compound vs simple, in one sentence

Simple interest pays you on your original money. Compound interest pays you on your original money and on the interest already added. The difference looks trivial in year one and enormous by year twenty, because each year’s growth becomes part of the base for the next.

£1,000 at 5%SimpleCompoundDifference
After 5 years£1,250£1,276£26
After 10 years£1,500£1,629£129
After 20 years£2,000£2,653£653

Now add monthly contributions. £100 a month for 10 years at 4.5% is £12,000 of your own money; the calculator will show the balance closer to £15,000. That extra is the growth, and it is the reason starting early beats starting big.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how long money takes to double. At 6%, about 12 years. At 3%, about 24. It is a rough tool, but useful for judging whether a rate is meaningful in your time frame.

The number that is missing: inflation

£10,000 in twenty years will not buy what £10,000 buys today. If inflation runs at 3% and your savings earn 3%, you have preserved purchasing power, not grown it. That is acceptable for an emergency fund and unacceptable for a 25-year investment pot. This is why long-term money usually needs exposure to growth assets — with the risk that comes with them.

Do not invest money you need within 3–5 years. Compounding rewards patience; a market fall in year two of a five-year goal has no time to recover. Keep short-term money in cash, and read the beginner investing guide before taking risk.

Fees compound too

A 1% annual platform fee does not sound like much, but it is charged on the whole balance every year and it compounds against you. On a £50,000 pot it is £500 in the first year and more thereafter. Check the ongoing charge figure on any fund before investing. Our compound interest guide works through the maths with UK rates, AER and fees.

Frequently asked questions

What rate should I use?

For cash savings, use the rate you can actually get today, not a promotional one. For investments, many planners use 4–6% after fees — and acknowledge that returns arrive unevenly.

Does this include tax?

No. UK savings interest may be taxable above your Personal Savings Allowance, and investments have their own rules. An ISA shelters returns, which is why the ISA comparison matters.

Why is my total lower than expected?

Check the rate, the number of years, and whether you entered a monthly contribution. The calculator compounds monthly and adds contributions at the end of each month.

Can compounding work against me?

Yes — credit card interest compounds. Use the debt planner to see how much a high APR costs over time.
