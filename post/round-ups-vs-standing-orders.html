Round-ups vs standing orders: which actually saves money?

Round-ups are the most pleasant way to save and one of the slowest. A standing order is less exciting and roughly ten times more effective. The honest answer is to use both, in the right proportions.

SaveGrow Lab Editorial Team08 November 20265 min read

In short

  • Round-ups typically move £5–£25 a month. Useful as a nudge, too small as a strategy.
  • A £25 standing order at 4% is about £3,700 after 10 years and £9,200 after 20.
  • Use both: the standing order does the work, the round-up catches the spare change.

What round-ups actually collect

A round-up app rounds each purchase to the nearest pound and moves the difference into savings. If you make 40 card purchases a month with an average round-up of 50p, that is £20. Some apps multiply the round-up or add a weekly bonus; without that, the amount is small by design.

That is not a reason to dismiss it. It is a reason to size it honestly. £20 a month is £240 a year, which is a real emergency-fund contribution for someone starting from zero. It is not a house deposit.

There is also a structural problem: round-ups are spent money. You are saving the shrapnel from purchases you already decided to make, rather than deciding how much to save and then spending the rest. That inverts the order that actually builds wealth.

What a standing order does

A standing order is a decision made once, in advance, in a known amount. It moves before you can negotiate with yourself. It is boring, visible and easy to verify — three properties that matter more than cleverness.

At the same 4%, a round-up habit averaging £15 a month is about £2,200 after ten years. The gap between that and £50 a month is not a better app; it is a decision.

How to use both

  1. Set the standing order at an amount you can keep in a bad month — £25 is a perfectly respectable start.
  2. Let round-ups run into the same pot if your bank offers them free. Treat the extra as a bonus, not the plan.
  3. Review once a year. When a pay rise lands, raise the standing order before the lifestyle does.
  4. Keep the pot separate. Round-ups that sit in your current account are not savings; they are a slightly larger balance you will spend.
The test: if you lost your round-up app tomorrow, would your saving rate change? If yes, the app was doing the work. A standing order is the version that survives a change of bank, phone or habit.

Are round-up apps worth it?

As a nudge, yes — especially if the app is free. As your main savings strategy, no. The amounts are too small and too tied to spending.

How much should I set as a standing order?

Start at 5% of take-home if you can, or £25 if you are starting from zero. Raise it at every pay review.

What if my bank does not offer weekly transfers?

Use a monthly transfer instead. The frequency matters far less than the automation and the separation.
